AFTER THE FOUR WAVES

What I’d take from the four waves.

01

People click when the founder is talking.

The best click in the test is Founder 2.3: “We couldn’t find the multivitamin we wanted for women our age. So we made it.” It only spent $44. Across all four waves, Founder is still the concept people click.

02

Science 3.1 is the one people put in the cart.

“More ingredients isn’t a better multivitamin.” Seven carts, eight checkouts, and the Wave 1 order. The biotin version and the third-party-tested version didn’t do that again.

03

These 20 ads made $37.

Science 3.1 came back as $2.99. The vitamins picture came back as $34. Founder 2.1 and Founder 2.5 each show a purchase with no dollars. The ad that’s actually been selling is 5.3, and it isn’t one of these 20.

WHAT BROUGHT THE PURCHASE

These are the ads that sold.

Whole Self 5.3 · not in the test

17 purchases · about $174

The picture says “I upgraded from my mom’s multivitamin.” The text says most multivitamins are made for everyone, and this one is for women in their 20s and 30s. That’s Aug 14 through Oct 7, the original test plus the scale campaign.

Wave 4 vitamins · inside the test

1 purchase · $34

The picture says “12 supplements every morning. One multivitamin. Two tablets a day.” It launched Oct 6. Besides Science 3.1’s $2.99, this is the only real dollar the 20 test ads made.

Science 3.1 · inside the test

1 purchase · $2.99

“More ingredients isn’t a better multivitamin.” Seven carts. Meta counted a purchase, and it was almost no money. The carts are the signal. The $2.99 isn’t.

THE LINES

What people are actually responding to.

What gets the click

The founder, talking about her age

“We couldn’t find the multivitamin we wanted for women our age. So we made it.” Best click in the test, and it only spent $44.

The founder, on gummies

“The world has enough gummy vitamins.” Strong click in Wave 3. They didn’t add it to a cart.

Beauty, on the biotin dose

“Seeing biotin on the label isn’t an effective dose.” Best click in Wave 2. 37 people landed on the site. Nobody added it to a cart.

UGC, “not a vitamin girl”

People click it. Almost nothing happens after that.

What gets closer to a sale

Science, fewer ingredients

“More ingredients isn’t a better multivitamin.” This is the Science line that got carts. Keep it.

The vitamins picture

“12 supplements every morning.” One $34 order, two days in. Leave it on.

The other Science lines didn’t get carts

“With biotin, the number matters” and “what we leave out matters” didn’t do what Science 3.1 did. Beauty’s biotin picture got the click and stopped there.

The new Whole Self lines didn’t get carts

Mom, “your routine grew up,” and “this one’s easy to keep” got people to the site and not into a cart. 5.3 is the Whole Self line that sells.

CREATIVE DIRECTION

What we should make next.

The copy

Keep “More ingredients isn’t a better multivitamin.” Keep “We couldn’t find it for women our age, so we made it.” Keep 5.3’s text: most multivitamins are made for everyone, this one is for women in their 20s and 30s. Take the draft note out of that live text.

The words on the picture

5.3 says it on the image: “I upgraded from my mom’s multivitamin.” The vitamins ad says it on the image: “12 supplements every morning.” Science 3.1’s headline is “See the 11.” The idea has to be readable on the picture. It can’t only live in the text underneath.

The visual

The pictures that sold are simple. Pills, or one jar in someone’s hand. Founder talking to camera is what gets the click. A biotin jar and a face covered in products get attention, and they haven’t sold. If the next round is meant to get orders, don’t lead with those.

What I’d do now

Leave 5.3 on. Give Founder 2.3 a real budget. It was the best click and it only spent $44. Keep Science on “more ingredients isn’t better.” Let Wave 4 keep running. The vitamins picture already has the $34. Two days isn’t enough to judge the other four.
